On the shelf

What should I
actually be taking?

Almost everybody asks me this one eventually, and the honest answer disappoints people. Until your food and your sleep are handled, no supplement moves the needle, and the ones that do are boring.

Then there’s the other half of it. The supplement industry is the Wild West, and plenty of what sits on a store shelf is fairy dust in a fancy tub, sold on a label you have no way to check. That’s the whole reason we picked one brand and stopped shopping.

We are a Thorne practitioner partner, and these four sit on the shelf at the gym year round. All four are NSF Certified for Sport, which means NSF tests every batch before it ships against the substances banned in sport. Not everything Thorne makes carries that mark, so I name the ones that do instead of waving at the catalog. If you take nothing else, these four are where I’d start most people. Everything after them is an adjustment.

And if you only ever buy one, buy the creatine. It’s the most researched sports supplement there is, it’s about fifty cents a day, and the only hard part is remembering to take it.

The other 23 hours

What desk do you
have at home?

An UPLIFT V3, and it’s the single most useful thing I have ever bought for my back. That’s the short answer. The part that matters more than the brand is why.

Before the rest of it: I’m on their four-leg model now, and UPLIFT sent me that desk and their walking pad free to review on my gear site. I bought my first V3 with my own money years before they knew who I was, and that’s the one that changed my mind about all of this.

Sitting is a slow death for a low back, and mine is on the shortlist for surgery eventually. I can back squat, deadlift, run and jump. Sitting for eight hours is the thing that wrecks me for days. And the most consistent thing I see in the people I coach who work at a desk is that same trio: cranky low back, tight hamstrings, and a chair they have never once thought about.

Standing for half the day gave me back days I stopped noticing. Better back days turned into better sleep, and better sleep turned into a better head. So if you have back problems and you’re on the fence, I’ll say it here the way I say it on the floor: it’s a nonnegotiable. Finance it if you have to. My only regret is the three to five years I spent researching instead of owning one.

The other half of that question is steps, and it takes two machines. An UPLIFT walking pad lives under the desk and quietly does the daily miles while I work. A MERACH T12 Pro lives in the garage for the real sessions, and the reason that one is a treadmill and not a second pad is the 15 percent auto incline. An incline walk puts my heart rate exactly where I want it for real zone 2 without beating anything up. Which brings me to the advice I took myself, and the one I give everybody who asks: don’t spend five thousand dollars to find out whether you’re the kind of person who uses a treadmill. Buy the one that makes the bet cheap, put a hundred miles on it, and upgrade when the miles demand it.

Who should skip both. If your desk time is genuinely an hour a day of email, a normal desk keeps your money. If you’re already a fast runner logging real miles, the T12 tops out at 7.5 mph and you should shop a tier up from the start. I’d rather tell you that now than sell you something you resent.

And one line I want to be clear about. If your back genuinely hurts, the sharp kind, the constant kind, the getting-worse kind, see a doctor before you see me. I’m a strength coach, not a physician, and nothing on this page is medical advice.

I wrote the desk and the T12 up properly, with photos, on Every Dad Carry, which is my gear review site: the full desk review and the treadmill review, including the belt-tension adjustment that catches people out. The walking pad review goes up once it has real miles on it. That site is mine, and some of the links on it are affiliate links, which means I may earn a commission if you buy through them.

The one hour

Why Rogue?
Why Concept2?

Because a commercial floor is the most honest equipment test there is. Whatever we buy gets used by everybody who trains here, every day, for years. Home-gym gear gets babied. Gym gear gets dropped, loaded wrong, left loaded, and put away in a hurry. Rogue and Concept2 are what I keep buying because I’ve stopped having to think about them, and that’s the whole philosophy: find the one thing that works, solve the problem, and never think about solving it again.

Is there anything better out there? For a commercial floor, honestly, not much, and nothing that changes the answer enough to matter. For your garage it’s a different question, and I’ll give you a different answer. Plenty of cheaper bars and racks are genuinely fine for one person training three days a week, and the money you save is better spent on coaching than on a bar that outlives you. Ask me about your specific setup and I will tell you where to spend and where not to.

Shoes, belts, sleeves, grips, chalk and ropes get asked about constantly and there’s nothing here yet. We are still testing and still sorting out who I actually want to send you to, so rather than pad this page out I’ll answer it in person. Ask a coach on the floor or send me a message. How something
gets on this page.

Three rules, and they are the reason this page is short.

01

I’ve actually used it

I own it, we stock it, or I’ve put real hours on it. Nothing gets recommended off a spec sheet.

02

It survives the no-commission test

I would tell you to buy it with nothing in it for me. If that’s not true, it doesn’t go up.

03

It comes off when I stop

If I stop using it, it leaves the page. A list like this is only worth reading if it can shrink.

That’s why two of the shelves above have nothing to sell you. I’d rather have one honest answer than six padded ones, and an empty shelf that tells you why is worth more than a full one that can’t.

less than the coaching.
