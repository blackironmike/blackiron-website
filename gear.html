The one hour

Why Rogue?
Why Concept2?

Because a commercial floor is the most honest equipment test there is. Whatever we buy gets used by everybody who trains here, every day, for years. Home-gym gear gets babied. Gym gear gets dropped, loaded wrong, left loaded, and put away in a hurry. Rogue and Concept2 are what I keep buying because I’ve stopped having to think about them, and that’s the whole philosophy: find the one thing that works, solve the problem, and never think about solving it again.

Is there anything better out there? For a commercial floor, honestly, not much, and nothing that changes the answer enough to matter. For your garage it’s a different question, and I’ll give you a different answer. Plenty of cheaper bars and racks are genuinely fine for one person training three days a week, and the money you save is better spent on coaching than on a bar that outlives you. Ask me about your specific setup and I will tell you where to spend and where not to.

Shoes, belts, sleeves, grips, chalk and ropes get asked about constantly and there’s nothing here yet. We are still testing and still sorting out who I actually want to send you to, so rather than pad this page out I’ll answer it in person. Ask a coach on the floor or send me a message. Same answer either way, including when the cheap option is fine.

How something
gets on this page.

Three rules, and they keep this page honest.

01

A coach would tell you to buy it

Everything here is the answer we give members when they ask. The ones marked Coach pick are things I own, we stock, or the gym runs on.

02

It survives the no-commission test

We would tell you to buy it with nothing in it for us. If that stops being true, it comes down.

03

The cheap option gets a fair shot

Where the budget version does the job, it’s on the shelf next to the expensive one. You don’t need the best of anything to train well.

The gear matters
less than the coaching.

All of this is downstream of showing up and being coached properly. The desk and the creatine are real, and they’re still worth less than the hours you spend with somebody watching your reps. If you want the part that actually moves the needle, start there.
