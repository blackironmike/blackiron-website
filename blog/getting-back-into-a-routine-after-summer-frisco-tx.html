The Kids Got Their Routine Back. Now Get Yours.

August 13, 2026 9 min read By Mike Manning

The first day of school, I did both drop-offs by myself.

My wife teaches elementary school. That morning she had to be at her own building early, getting her room ready to meet her class for the first time. So the run was mine. Our two-year-old to pre-K, then our five-year-old to his first day of kindergarten.

I got a taste of what her every other morning looks like. I have a new respect for it.

People had told me the first kindergarten drop-off is harder on the parent than it is on the kid. I nodded along like I understood that. I did not. He walked in fine. I sat in the truck a little longer than I needed to.

Then I drove home, and the house was quiet. Not gone-for-an-hour quiet. The real kind. It has not sounded like that since May.

Your kids just had their routine handed back to them. Bedtimes, wake-ups, meals, the whole structure, and they did not have to decide a thing. Yours is sitting right there too. It just needs someone to claim it.

That quiet is a fork in the road, and I have watched people take the wrong branch for thirteen years.

Half your year, gone to the same twenty weeks

From this week to January 1st is about twenty weeks. That is thirty-eight percent of your year.

Here is what usually happens to it. Not dramatically. Nobody sits down and decides to let the fall go. It just goes. Football starts. Practices stack up. October brings the first round of candy. Thanksgiving is a week, not a day. December is a write-off by the tenth, and somewhere in there the workouts quietly became a thing you used to do.

Then on January 1st you make a resolution to undo it, and the first six weeks of that resolution go to getting back to where you already were in August.

Add those up, because the number is worse than it feels. Twenty weeks of drift plus six weeks of clawing back is twenty-six weeks. Half your year. Every year. Spent either losing ground or recovering ground you already had.

Which leaves the other half to actually build something. And that is the generous version, the one where you do not run the same play again next August.

Most people are not losing a discipline fight. They are running a calendar that has them out of rhythm more often than in it, and then taking it personally when the results match. Half the year off tempo, out of routine, and well stocked with reasons why this particular season is not the one.

August is the one month of the year that hands the calendar back to you.

January isn’t the fresh start it looks like

I am not going to knock anyone who starts in January. We take those members every year and they do great.

But be honest about what a January start actually is. You are beginning in the coldest, darkest, busiest stretch of the year, in a gym packed with people who will not be there in March, and you are not starting from zero. You are starting from twenty weeks of drift. The first six weeks go to getting back to where you already were in August.

You can simply not do that.

What twenty weeks is actually worth

Twenty weeks is not a challenge or a shred. It is a real training block, long enough to run several full cycles of programmed strength work instead of a random string of hard days.

Most people notice their energy shift in the first couple of weeks. Body composition changes tend to show up somewhere between four and eight. Twenty weeks is well past both, and it lands you on January 1st with the hardest part already behind you.

That is the part worth sitting with. Everyone else spends the holidays going backwards. If you started in August, the holidays cost you a couple of easy weeks off a base you already built. You come out the other side maintaining. They come out starting.

Same December. Completely different January.

Lock in, don’t check out

It is not about more willpower. It is five unglamorous decisions:

  • Pick the hour that survives your worst week. Not your best week. If Thursdays fall apart, pick a time that still works on a bad Thursday. We run six class times a day for exactly this reason. Look at the class schedule and choose for the version of you that is tired.
  • Put it in the calendar like a drop-off. You do not skip a pickup because you are tired. Give the workout the same status and stop renegotiating it every morning.
  • Commit to a number you would be embarrassed to miss. Three days a week you actually keep beats five you abandon in October. Consistency compounds. Intensity does not.
  • Plan for the week it gets hard. It will. We put the lighter week of every cycle where your life gets heavier. Back-to-school week was a deload on purpose. The gym should bend around your season, not add to it.
  • Do not start alone. Every member here starts with Foundations, one-on-one coaching before your first class, so you learn the movements at your own pace instead of trying to keep up with a room.

And if none of those six work in a given week, the floor still does. Every hour of every day here is either a class or open gym. If a class is not on the floor, the floor is open, which means there is no hour of the week you cannot come in and train. The full open gym schedule is on the schedule page. It comes with the Unlimited and the open gym memberships, and it is why people here stop treating a missed class as a missed week.

And if it has been years rather than months, that is a different starting line and it deserves its own approach. I wrote about coming back after a long break separately.

The person who shows up on January 1st

Someone is going to walk in here on the second of January, look around at a full floor, and feel like they are behind.

It does not have to be you. You could be the one who is four months in by then. Who knows where the chalk is. Who has a coach that knows your name and your bad shoulder and what you are working toward. Everyone else will be making a resolution about the thing you already did.

The window for that is not January. It is right now, in the quiet after drop-off, before the fall fills it in on its own.

Come talk to me. Twenty minutes, no cost, and no pitch. If we are not the right fit, I will tell you. Bring the week you actually live, kids and job and old injuries and all of it, and we will build the plan around that.

Twenty weeks from now, you will be glad you used the quiet.
