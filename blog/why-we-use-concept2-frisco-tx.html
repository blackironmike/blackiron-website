Why Every Rower, SkiErg and Bike on Our Floor Is Concept2

November 23, 2026 5 min read By Mike Manning

Every rower, SkiErg and bike on our floor has the same name on the side: Concept2. People ask why we didn’t mix in something cheaper. Short answer: in a gym, cheap is the expensive option.

And we just doubled down. For the new space next door, opening in January, we ordered 10 RowErgs, 10 BikeErgs and 5 SkiErgs. Twenty-five more machines, same name on the side.

Here’s why, what it means for you in class, and which one I’d buy first if you’re setting up a garage.

A Decade of Commercial Use, Very Little Maintenance

A gym machine doesn’t get used like a home machine. It gets used every class, by every level, and again in open gym seven days a week. Somebody sprints on it at 5am. Somebody else does a slow recovery row on it at noon. Somebody drops the handle.

Some of the Concept2s on our floor have been taking that for well over a decade. Thousands of classes, thousands of members, and they’ve asked very little of us in return. Keep them clean, do the basic upkeep, and they keep going.

Built to Be Fixed, Not Replaced

Concept2 is a Vermont company that’s been building rowing machines for more than forty years, and the machines are simple on purpose: a flywheel, a fan, a chain or a belt, and a monitor. There’s not much to go wrong, and when something does wear out, you order that part and swap it in. You replace the part, not the machine.

That matters to me as an owner. A machine that can be fixed in an afternoon is a machine that’s back on the floor for the next class. It should matter to you as a member too, because a broken rower means you’re on the wrong machine for the workout.

The Machine Only Goes as Hard as You Do

All three run on air resistance. The flywheel pushes against air, so the harder you pull, push or pedal, the harder it pushes back. Ease off and it eases off with you.

That’s why they fit the way we coach. Three levels, one floor. Someone in their first week and someone training for a HYROX race can row side by side in the same workout, and each one gets the resistance that fits them without touching a dial. There’s a damper lever on the side, and we’ll show you where to set it. Your effort does the rest.

They’re also low impact. No pounding through the knees and ankles. If you can’t afford to get hurt because your kids expect you on the floor with them after work, that counts for a lot.

One Monitor, Three Machines

The RowErg, SkiErg and BikeErg all use the same monitor, the PM5. Learn it once and you know all three. Pace, watts, calories, distance and time, laid out the same way on every machine.

When the whiteboard says 500 meters or 15 calories, you already know where to look and what you’re chasing.

Your Numbers Mean Something

So many gyms, teams and races use these machines that a Concept2 number is one you can compare. A 2,000 meter row on our floor is the same 2,000 meters on a Concept2 anywhere else, including the one in your garage.

That’s what makes testing and retesting honest. Row it at the start of a cycle, row it again at the end, and the change is yours, not a friendlier machine. Concept2 also runs a free online logbook if you want to keep track of every piece you do.

It’s also why HYROX races use Concept2 rowers and SkiErgs. We’re a HYROX affiliate, and the machines you train on here are the same ones you’ll see on race day.

What Each One Is For

  • RowErg. The full-body one. Legs, back and arms in one stroke, and it teaches you to drive with your legs before you pull with your arms. The best first machine for most people.
  • SkiErg. You stand and drive two handles down from overhead, hinging at the hips. Mostly upper body and core. Great conditioning on a day your legs are already cooked from squats.
  • BikeErg. Seated, smooth, and the easiest on the joints. A good option on days your knees or back want a break, and anyone can go hard on it without worrying about technique.

If You’re Buying One for Home

Some of you train in the garage on the days you can’t make it in. If you’re going to buy one machine, here’s how I’d think about it.

  1. Start with the RowErg. The most training for the money, and it’s the one you already know from class. It also splits into two pieces and stands up on end, so it doesn’t take over the garage.
  2. Add the BikeErg if your joints need it. If running or rowing bothers your back or knees on certain days, the bike is the easy-on-everything option.
  3. Add the SkiErg if you’re training for HYROX. It’s a race station, and it’s the hardest of the three to get good at without practice.

These aren’t the cheapest machines out there. But ours have run for more than ten years of daily classes, and a garage asks a lot less of a machine than a gym does. They hold their value if you ever sell, and if a part wears out, you fix it. Buy once.

Before you buy, come row one with us and ask a coach to check your setup and your stroke. Ten minutes on the right technique is worth more than any machine.
