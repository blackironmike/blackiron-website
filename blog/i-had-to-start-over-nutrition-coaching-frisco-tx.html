I Own a Gym. I Still Had to Start Over.

August 2, 2026 9 min read By Mike Manning

October 2024 is where this starts. Something was wrong, and working out what it was turned into a health scare, then surgery in February, and then a recovery I am only now getting to the end of.

I own a gym. I have coached for more than twenty years. And for the better part of two years I have not been able to train the way I used to.

I am writing this down because a lot of people end up here one way or another, and most of them assume the guy who owns the gym has it figured out.

The part nobody expects a gym owner to admit

I was a wrestler. Then a powerlifter. Then a CrossFitter. I served in the Marine Corps. I have spent my whole adult life around training, and thirteen years coaching other people through exactly this.

None of it mattered when I could not do it.

By the time I came out of surgery in February I was the least healthy I had been in my adult life. My energy did not support the job. It did not support two boys under six who want to wrestle on the living room floor at seven in the morning. I looked in the mirror and did not recognize the guy standing there, and I felt like a fraud walking onto my own floor.

Recovery is slow and it does not care about your training history. I could not lift my way out of it. I could not run my way out of it. That is a healing timeline, not a mindset problem, and for a long stretch there was nothing I could do to speed it up.

Which left one lever. If I could not train, I could still eat like it mattered. So that is where I put everything.

Knowing what to do and having a way to actually do it are two different problems. I had the first one solved. I had nothing for the second.

Every app I tried solved the wrong problem

I tried them. Most of them. Some I had used years earlier and quit. Some I quit again.

Logging was tedious enough that I would skip a day, then skip three, then stop. The numbers were inconsistent depending on which entry I picked. And none of them could answer the only question that actually mattered to me: what happens after this?

That is the same hole in every six-week challenge I have ever run, done, or watched. And I have run a lot of them. They work. People get real results in six weeks, and that matters, because it proves to them that nutrition is the lever they have not been pulling.

Then week seven arrives and there is nothing there. What they did was not sustainable, they know it, and there is no next phase. So they go back to guessing, back to shooting from the hip, back to living on bars and shakes and smoothies. Those foods are not evil. They are just not a plan.

What I actually needed was a plan with a next phase

So I built one, and I ran it on myself first.

Fuel. Eight weeks eating at what my body actually burns. Not a diet. This phase exists so you learn what your body needs before you ask it to change. Most people are shocked by how much they get to eat, and a lot of body composition change happens right here, before any deficit starts.

Cut. A deliberate deficit with a start and an end date. Protein goes up, which helps with fullness and costs you more calories just to digest it. Fat stays roughly where it is, because that is where your hormones and your energy live. Carbohydrates come down, because carbohydrates are the lever. They are the gas in the tank, and the tank should match the mileage of the day.

Fuel again. This is the phase everyone skips, and skipping it is why people rebound. You just changed your body. Less fat, more muscle. That body does not need what the old one needed, so we measure again and rebuild the targets around who you are now.

Build. The winter arc. A controlled surplus with the intention of adding muscle, leaning into the season instead of fighting it. Muscle is expensive to maintain, which is exactly the point. It raises what you burn at rest, so next year’s cut starts from a better place.

Then it repeats. That is the part that took me twenty years to really understand. The physiques you follow online were not built in a season or a year. They were built in a decade of doing this on purpose.

The numbers, including the one I did not expect

From October 2024 to today:

Lean mass112.9 lb119.0 lb+6.1 lb
Body fat24.1%14.5%−9.6 pts

Visceral fat index84−4

Measured on the same scale throughout, October 2024 to August 2026.

Look at the first and third numbers together, because this is the whole point. The scale moved about ten pounds. Fat moved seventeen. Muscle went up six.

If I had judged that stretch by the scale alone, I would have concluded it was barely working and quit somewhere in the middle. That is exactly what happens to people every day. The scale is the least useful number in the room and it is the only one most of us look at.

The visceral fat number is the one I actually care about most, and it has nothing to do with how I look. That is the number that says I get to be around and moving for my boys. Everything else is a bonus.

Measured, not calculated

Every macro calculator on the internet, including the free one we publish, is an estimate. It uses your height, weight, age, and a guess at your activity, and it gets you in the neighborhood. It does not know you.

What actually worked was measuring. Once you have enough logged days and enough weigh-ins, your real burn falls out of the math: what you ate, against what your weight actually did. No formula, no guessing at an activity multiplier. Just your own data telling you what your body is really doing, and updating as your body changes.

That number is what my targets get built from now, and it is why the plan kept working instead of stalling out at week six.

Why I built the software instead of buying it

Because nothing out there did all three jobs at once.

If the app is miserable for the client, they stop logging, and their coach is flying blind. If it is thin on the coach side, the coach cannot see what is happening and cannot help, and the client ends up blaming the coach for something that was really a tooling problem. And if the whole thing sells directly to the consumer for pocket change, no coach can build a real service on top of it.

Solve one, break the others. So I built FuelPath to solve all three, with twenty years of coaching poured into it. It is the app I could not find.

To be straight about the order this happened in: October 2024 is where the measurements start, February is when I had surgery, and the recovery has run more than a year since. Nutrition is the thing I could control that whole time, and it is what moved those numbers. The app came out of that stretch, not before it. I have only been running my own training on FuelPath seriously for about three months, and I am still finishing the recovery it came from.

It runs in Black Iron colors for our members, and there is a real coach on the other end of it. The technology handles the tracking, the trends, and the reminders. It does not do the coaching. That is still a person who reads your check-in and knows your name.

The habits fill themselves in from what you already logged, because the fastest way to make someone quit is to make them do the same work twice.

What this means if you train here

We have fifteen clients running this right now and the results have been good enough that we are rolling it out gym-wide shortly.

Here is the belief underneath all of it. Nutrition is the foundation. If you have a fueling problem, you have a problem you cannot outtrain, outrun, or outwork, and I proved that on myself the hard way. You can have a perfect training program and undo all of it in the other twenty-three hours.

None of this requires anything exotic. Whole foods win, almost every time. Chicken, rice, broccoli, salmon, potatoes, fruit. Food with one ingredient. A lot of what gets sold as health food is engineered to be easy to overeat and to leave you hungry an hour later, which is good for the company selling it and bad for you. Volume, protein, and food you actually recognize will beat it every time.

If any of this sounds like where you are

I am not going to pretend any of this was fast. It was not. It was the long way around, and it was the only way that held.
